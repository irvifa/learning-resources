# Neuroscience

Courses on brain structure and function, cognitive neuroscience, computational neuroscience, and behavior.

> **Access notes**
> - MIT OCW: entirely free, no registration required (archived materials)
> - Princeton: course materials freely available on instructor's lab site

---

## MIT

### [Brain Structure and Its Origins (9.14)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-14-brain-structure-and-its-origins-spring-2014/)
- **Platform:** MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Level:** Undergraduate
- **Instructor:** Gerald E. Schneider
- **Description:** Vertebrate brain structure from comparative and developmental perspectives — neuroanatomy, motor and sensory pathways, motivational systems, habit formation, and cognitive functions; includes brain dissection lab.

### [Sensory Systems (9.04)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-04-sensory-systems-fall-2013/)
- **Platform:** MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Level:** Undergraduate
- **Instructors:** Peter H. Schiller, M. Christian Brown
- **Description:** How the nervous system processes sensory information — visual pattern, color and depth perception, auditory responses and sound localization, and somatosensory perception, drawing on mammalian physiology, anatomy, and behavioral studies.

### [Cellular Neurobiology (7.29J)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/7-29j-cellular-neurobiology-spring-2012/)
- **Platform:** MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Level:** Undergraduate
- **Instructors:** Troy Littleton, William Quinn
- **Description:** Nervous system structure and function at the cellular level — biophysical properties of excitable cells, synaptic transmission, neurochemistry, neurodevelopment, and information integration in the visual system.

### [Neuroscience and Behavior (9.01)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-01-neuroscience-and-behavior-fall-2003/)
- **Platform:** MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Level:** Undergraduate
- **Instructor:** Gerald E. Schneider
- **Description:** Functional neuroanatomy, neurophysiology, sensory and motor systems, sleep, motivation, emotional responses, and neural processes in learning and memory.

### [Statistics for Brain and Cognitive Science (9.07)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-07-statistics-for-brain-and-cognitive-science-fall-2016/)
- **Platform:** MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Level:** Undergraduate
- **Instructor:** Emery N. Brown
- **Description:** Analytical foundations for experimental neuroscience data — probability theory, statistical inference (Bayesian, frequentist, Monte Carlo), hypothesis testing, and linear models including regression and ANOVA.

### [Research Topics in Neuroscience (9.95-A)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-95-a-research-topics-in-neuroscience-january-iap-2003/)
- **Platform:** MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Level:** Graduate (seminar)
- **Instructor:** Peter H. Schiller
- **Description:** Faculty research presentations covering neurons to neural networks, prefrontal cortex, hippocampal memory, motor learning, visual attention, and brain wiring.

### [Introduction to Computational Neuroscience (9.29J)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-29j-introduction-to-computational-neuroscience-spring-2004/)
- **Platform:** MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Level:** Undergraduate
- **Instructor:** Sebastian Seung
- **Description:** Mathematical foundations of neural coding and dynamics — linear systems, information theory, reinforcement learning, Hodgkin-Huxley models, and synaptic transmission.

### [Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience (9.S915)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-s915-developmental-cognitive-neuroscience-spring-2012/)
- **Platform:** MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Level:** Graduate
- **Instructors:** Susan Carey, Rebecca Saxe
- **Description:** Neuroscience methods applied to cognitive development in infants and children — face recognition, language, executive function, object representation, number, and theory of mind.

### [Introduction to Computational Neuroscience with Neuroblox (RES.9-009)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-9-009-introduction-to-computational-neuroscience-with-neuroblox-january-iap-2025/)
- **Platform:** MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Level:** Graduate / Non-Credit
- **Instructors:** Lilianne Mujica-Parodi, Helmut Strey, Haris Organtzidis
- **Description:** Hands-on computational neuroscience using Neuroblox and Julia — simulate neural models from single neurons to complex circuits with synaptic plasticity, calibrated against real neural data.

### [Neural Basis of Learning and Memory (9.03)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-03-neural-basis-of-learning-and-memory-fall-2007/)
- **Platform:** MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Level:** Undergraduate
- **Instructors:** Matt Wilson, Suzanne Corkin
- **Description:** Interplay between cellular/molecular storage mechanisms and cognitive neuroscience of memory, with focus on hippocampal function in learning across human and animal models.

### [Neuroscience of Morality (9.46)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-46-neuroscience-of-morality-fall-2017/)
- **Platform:** MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Level:** Undergraduate
- **Instructor:** Rebecca Saxe
- **Description:** How the brain enables moral decision-making — neural mechanisms underlying moral evaluation, right/wrong judgments, and punishment behavior.

### [Brain and Cognitive Sciences Computational Tutorial Series (RES.9-008)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-9-008-brain-and-cognitive-sciences-computational-tutorials/)
- **Platform:** MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Level:** Non-Credit
- **Instructors:** Emily Mackevicius, Jenelle Feather, Nhat Le, Fernanda De La Torre Romo, Greta Tuckute, Ajani Stewart, Sol Markman
- **Description:** Ongoing seminar series (2015–present) on computational methods across neuroscience, cognitive science, and AI — each session includes lecture, interactive exercises, and office hours. Resources include videos, slides, code, and datasets.

### [Perception (9.35)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/9-35-perception-spring-2024/)
- **Platform:** MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Level:** Undergraduate
- **Instructor:** Josh McDermott
- **Description:** How the senses transform physical stimuli into neural signals — emphasis on hearing and vision, with coverage of touch, taste, and smell; uses optical and auditory illusions alongside psychophysical experiments.

### [BCS Tutorials: Optimizing the Full Stack for Generative Image and Video Models (RES.9-010)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-9-010-bcs-tutorials-optimizing-the-full-stack-for-generative-image-and-video-models-fall-2025/)
- **Platform:** MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Level:** Non-Credit
- **Instructor:** Sayak Paul (Hugging Face)
- **Description:** Optimization strategies for diffusion models used in image and video generation — architecture design, hardware efficiency, and inference techniques. Part of a BCS seminar series bridging neuroscience, cognitive science, and AI.

## Princeton

### [Sensation & Perception (PSY 345 / NEU 325)](https://pillowlab.princeton.edu/teaching/sp2017/)
- **Platform:** Princeton Pillow Lab
- **Level:** Undergraduate
- **Instructor:** Jonathan Pillow
- **Description:** Scientific study of sensation and perception across vision, audition, touch, smell, and taste — integrating philosophy, physics, computer science, neuroscience, and psychology to trace how physical stimuli become percepts in the brain.
