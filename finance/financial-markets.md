# Financial Markets

Courses on markets structure, securities, investments, portfolio theory, and behavioral finance.

> **Access notes**
> - MIT OCW: entirely free, no registration required (archived materials)
> - Yale Open Courses: entirely free, no registration required

---

## MIT

### [Finance Theory I (15.401)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-401-finance-theory-i-fall-2008/)
- **Platform:** MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Level:** Graduate
- **Instructor:** Andrew Lo
- **Description:** Capital markets and financial intermediaries, asset valuation, fixed-income securities, equities, portfolio selection, equilibrium pricing of risky assets, efficient markets theory, and an introduction to derivatives.

### [Investments (15.433)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-433-investments-spring-2003/)
- **Platform:** MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Level:** Graduate
- **Instructor:** Reto Gallati
- **Description:** Financial theory and empirical evidence for investment decisions — portfolio theory, CAPM, arbitrage pricing theory, market efficiency, investment performance evaluation, and behavioral finance.

### [Financial Management (15.414)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-414-financial-management-summer-2003/)
- **Platform:** MIT OpenCourseWare
- **Level:** Graduate
- **Instructor:** Jonathan Lewellen
- **Description:** Corporate finance and capital markets — valuation of assets and derivatives, risk and return, capital structure, and distribution policies, grounded in empirical research.

## Yale

### [Financial Markets (ECON 252)](https://oyc.yale.edu/economics/econ-252)
- **Platform:** Yale Open Courses
- **Level:** Undergraduate
- **Instructor:** Robert J. Shiller
- **Description:** Ideas, methods, and institutions that allow society to manage risks and foster enterprise — securities, insurance, banking, behavioral finance, and the future of financial markets.
